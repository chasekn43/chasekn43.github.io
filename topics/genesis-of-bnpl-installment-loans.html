The Genesis of Modern BNPL: Installment Loans Disguised as Pay-in-Four

The very first instance of a modern Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) fintech company structuring its business around 3-, 6-, and 12-month interest-bearing installment loans was the launch of Affirm in 2012.

“The minute you stray from the pay-in-four, all of a sudden you’re in installment loan land,” Lisa Gill, an investigative reporter for Consumer Reports, told Straight Arrow.
“Affirm…is especially aggressive in offering interest-bearing loans, consumer advocates told Straight Arrow. Affirm charged interest on 71% of the gross merchandise volume of its loans, according to its most recent quarterly earnings report.”
“Affirm reported in a regulatory filing that 13% of the gross merchandise volume of its monthly installment loans did not carry interest in the first three months of 2026. In other words, 87% of loans did carry interest.”

Standard Affirm loans include finance charges that can reach up to 36% APR.

“On the BNPL side, many short‑term plans advertise 'interest‐free' if you pay on schedule, making them appear more affordable. However, that doesn't always tell the full story: longer‑term BNPL loans have been documented with APRs up to about 36.99%, significantly higher than many credit‑card rates in those specific cases.”

The Regulatory Consensus and Dispute Vulnerability

Credit cards offer strong, federally mandated purchase protection, extended warranties, and easy chargeback/fraud dispute options. Resolving a dispute or return through Affirm can be much more difficult because they act as a third-party lender.

🏛️ The Regulatory Consensus

The current legal consensus acknowledges that while BNPL's simple-interest structure is safer than a credit card's compounding trap, the lack of uniform consumer dispute protections remains a major vulnerability for shoppers.
